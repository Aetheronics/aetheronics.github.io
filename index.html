02 — The radiotransistor

A BC547, rewritten in horns and plasma.

Emitter maps to the source horn. Collector maps to the drain horn. Base maps to a 60° tungsten needle. The silicon die is replaced by a 0.3 mm atmospheric plasma volume.

03 — Materials

Engineered for the field.

Horns

Silver-plated brass or aluminium. Conical 40 mm aperture to 8 mm throat. Precision flare for C-band match.

Gate tip

Tungsten–rhenium or thoriated tungsten. 60° needle taper for field emission and controlled ignition.

Dielectrics

Alumina (Al₂O₃) or PTFE. High-voltage isolation and low RF loss around the micro-chamber.

Chamber

Hermetic micro-volume. Pure Ar or Ar–N₂ at 1 atm. No vacuum pump on the device itself.

04 — Why radiowaves

The field can be shaped, gated, and stored in ways charge never could.

Switching and modulation occur across the microwave spectrum without the thermal and transit-time limits of a semiconductor junction. The active region is a plasma volume that exists only when the gate asks it to.

Horn and needle structures scale with wavelength. The same architecture applies from C-band through millimeter-wave. Aetheronics is building the first devices of that family — starting with the atmospheric plasma-gated RF attenuator and switch.
